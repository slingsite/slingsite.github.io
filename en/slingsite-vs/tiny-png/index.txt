<p>TinyPNG relies on a server-side model. You upload your files to their cloud, their servers process them, and you download the optimized version.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Simplicity:</strong> It’s famous for being extremely easy to use.</li>
<li><strong>Integrations:</strong> Offers plugins for Photoshop and various CMS platforms.</li>
</ul>
<h3>The "Pain Points"</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The "Quota Wall":</strong> The most frustrating part of TinyPNG is the limit. Once you hit 20 images, you have to refresh or stop. For a project with 50+ images, this becomes a major bottleneck.</li>
<li><strong>File Size Caps:</strong> The 5MB limit on the free tier prevents you from optimizing high-resolution photography or raw assets.</li>
<li><strong>Privacy Concerns:</strong> Since your images are sent to a third-party server, it’s not the best choice for proprietary or sensitive assets that shouldn't leave your machine.</li>
</ul>
